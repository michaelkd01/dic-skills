# Verbatim Exemplars ... Michael's Sent Mail and Slack (July 2026 sample)

All examples below are Michael's actual sent messages. Pattern-match against these. Do not quote them back to recipients ... they are style references only.

## 1. Minimal acknowledgements (the default reply)

- "Hi, happy to sign. Thanks"
- "Yeah, I was thinking exactly that."
- "Thanks for the reminder, that's done"
- "Ok will do, cheers"
- "Ok no problem"
- "Yes please"
- "Ok thanks Tom" / "Ok, thanks Tom. Will remove the meeting."

Lesson: if the substance is agreement or completion, that IS the message. No preamble, no restatement.

## 2. Action + owner + proposed default

- "Let me check with the guys. What time in the morning works for you? Remembering Jeremy's two hours behind. 10am?"
- "Hi, the guys are ok with an 11:00 start. I'll update the invite shortly."
- "Thanks all. I'll update the invite."
- "Hi Nick, any chance of pushing this back to 1pm?"
- "Can we shift our one-to-one to Friday, same time?"
- "And regarding the regular catch-ups, does 10 a.m. Wednesday work for you?"

Lesson: never leave scheduling or next steps open-ended. Propose the specific default.

## 3. Engineered questions and the pragmatic fallback

Structured decision reply (Slack, to Ken):
> "Hi Ken, thanks for the update. My thoughts are...
> - Can we make our reports public but then host them on a site behind auth?
> - If the above isn't possible, it's absolutely fine continue sharing the PDFs if that's simpler, but could we just put them in one PDF?
> - Yes we still want all the charts either online or in a PDF
> - And yes forwarding the weekly email with comments and summary is also fine
>
> So essentially if it's all too hard, let's just have Khai add comments and forward out the report we already have."

Binary question with pre-answered follow-up:
> "Quick question about the process... If, when I choose to access these shares later, do they have to be put directly into my name, or can they go into my family trust?"

Technical ask with expected-vs-actual data (Slack, to Ken):
> "1. FUNDED DEALS weekly: what filter beyond Deal Status=Funded + week bucket? (We get 4 deals / $415k where the pack shows 3 / $390k for w/e 19 Jul)"

Lesson: every question carries its own answer options, verification data, or escape hatch.

## 4. Challenge with low heat + self-attributed error

Correcting the board record:
> "Hi guys, just for clarity, we explicitly agreed when I presented options that refinancing out the notes was not achievable while raising for the unit trust, and that all new funds should be directed..."

Querying the CEO's model:
> "...it looks like super has been pulled out of the employee expense lines. Has it shifted elsewhere or am I misinterpreting the calculation?"

Chasing missing docs without accusation:
> "Hi, have you shared the docs for the board meeting? I don't think I've seen them, but there's a chance I've missed it."

Pushing back on a vendor price:
> "The feedback was that while they like the service, they don't think they can justify the $1500 per month minimum given the likely low volume"

Challenging a sales claim, direct:
> "Hi Will, We only recently renewed our Salesforce contract. What renewal are you talking about?"

## 5. Owning mistakes and gaps

- "Sorry mate, I authorised that just after Gary posted it, just got sidetracked and didn't add the settlement message. Will do that now."
- "I know I off-handedly dropped that on you!"
- "I'll need to run a search. Not sure how to do it, but can get it on the list for when I'm back." ... then ten minutes later: "That was easier than I thought. Here's the list."

## 6. Delegation with re-entry clause

> "Hi Will, It's probably best to pick up with our Head of Engineering, Ken (cc'd). He is handling all of the tech-related renewals. Ken... Feel free to pull me in if you need me. Thanks, Michael"

## 7. Alignment verification

> "Separate to the budget work, and just so I know we're on the same page, can you share in your words what you think the take-aways were from our chat yesterday? I'm keen to make this part of our regular catch-ups moving forward."

# Systematic drift to watch for (top 5)

1. **Context re-explanation** ... establishing facts the recipient already owns. Delete everything inferable.
2. **Corporate plural** ... "we'd like to" where he'd say "I". Reserve "we" for genuine board/company positions.
3. **Missing ask mechanics** ... drafts that end on information. His end on an action with owner and proposed default.
4. **Formality floor too high** ... treating every professional as semi-formal. Tier by relationship depth.
5. **Length** ... target 40 to 60% shorter than a "thorough" draft, cutting narration, never content.
